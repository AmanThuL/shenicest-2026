# Our 2025 advanced technical content recap: Upskill your game development with these resources

<span class="text-gray-900 dark:text-gray-100 pb-1 loco-caption-lg-semibold">THOMAS KROGH-JACOBSEN / UNITY TECHNOLOGIES</span><span class="text-gray-700 dark:text-gray-300 tracking-normal loco-text-body-xs-semibold">Senior Technical Content Marketing Manager</span>

<span class="mr-2">Dec 16, 2025</span>

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

In this blog we wrap up the year by giving you an overview of new technical resources released in 2025. We hope that you find these helpful and that you’ll bookmark them for the holidays and beyond.

Before I dive into this technical content year in review, bear with me in sharing a small anecdote from [Unite Barcelona](https://unity.com/events/unite) in November. I had the pleasure of helping out at the main booth, where my colleagues and I spent most of our time showcasing the latest UI Toolkit and 2D samples. As always, it was a great opportunity to talk to a lot of people from our community, from university students to larger studios, as well as Unity YouTubers like [Cristina Makes Games](https://www.youtube.com/@ChristinaCreatesGames), [CodeMonkey](https://www.youtube.com/@CodeMonkeyUnity) and [GameDevGuide](https://www.youtube.com/c/GameDevGuide).

  

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

A lot of you told us you’re already using the e-books and samples, or watching our video tutorials, and that is something the team and I greatly appreciate.

There was also plenty of feedback on what could be even better. A lot of you wanted to get started with UI Toolkit but told us that the learning curve is steep; I have a few links to help you with that further below.

We also heard that some of you simply didn’t know certain resources were available, which tells us we need to do a better job of making things discoverable. The content lives in many different places, and it can be hard to track it all down. We also hear from you that you want more advanced content on [Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/). That’s another area we really want to improve in 2026. More on that later.

For now, I thought a good place to start would be a wrap-up blog post, similar to what we did [last year](https://unity.com/blog/2024-technical-content-roundup), highlighting some of the newer resources you might have missed.

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

## Level up your skills now and in 2026

### Get started with UI Toolkit

There’s good news for those of you looking to move from Unity UI (UGUI) to UI Toolkit: We have several resources today updated for Unity 6, and more are planned for 2026 to help you hit the ground running.

If you are new to UI Toolkit, I recommend starting with our e-book *[Create scalable and performant UI with UI Toolkit in Unity 6](https://unity.com/resources/scalable-performant-ui-uitoolkit-unity-6).* From there, you can move on to one or both of our free sample projects on the Unity Asset Store: [QuizU](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/essentials/tutorial-projects/quizu-a-ui-toolkit-sample-268492) and [Dragon Crashers – UI Toolkit](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/essentials/tutorial-projects/dragon-crashers-ui-toolkit-sample-project-231178).

The Dragon Crashers - UI Toolkit Sample project showcases UI Toolkit and UI Builder workflows for runtime games.

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

If you are one of the customers we talked to in Barcelona who are excited that we now support World Space UI, shaders, and SVG, check out the [Bagel Game project on Github](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/BagelGame). It’s a nice, silly little game that showcases how to use all of these. Finally, the [documentation pages](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/UIE-examples.html) also have a ton of examples that you might find helpful.

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

## More for programmers

### Make your code base more scalable and testable

Earlier this year I helped update our *[Unity C# code style guide for Unity 6](https://unity.com/resources/c-sharp-style-guide-unity-6)*[.](https://unity.com/resources/c-sharp-style-guide-unity-6) There’s no single “right” way to format Unity C# code, but aligning on a consistent style makes it much easier to build a clean, readable, and scalable codebase. Since then, I’ve been experimenting with adapting the original style guide into my own and applying it in my work. I wrote an [article here](https://discussions.unity.com/t/creating-code-style-instructions-for-github-copilot/1694999) with a few tips and tricks, which I hope can be useful for others.

We also expanded our [SOLID character controller project and article series](https://discussions.unity.com/t/tips-for-writing-cleaner-code-that-scales-in-unity-part-3-adding-a-modular-interaction-system-using-interfaces/1581732), in partnership with Peter from [Sunny Valley Studio](https://www.youtube.com/@SunnyValleyStudio), demonstrating how to apply general object-oriented programming principles to make your code more readable, well-organized, and maintainable.

Tips for writing cleaner, scalable code in Unity is a five-part article series created in partnership with Sunny Valley Studio, which focuses on helping you write better code for your Unity projects.

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

Finally, we updated some of our popular e-books for Unity 6, including our guides on [ScriptableObjects](https://unity.com/resources/create-modular-game-architecture-scriptableobjects-unity-6), [Unity DOTS](https://unity.com/resources/dots-concepts-features-samples-resources-unity-6), and [multiplayer networking](https://unity.com/resources/ultimate-guide-advanced-multiplayer-networking). Our [ScriptableObjects Paddle Ball project](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/templates/tutorials/scriptableobjects-paddle-ball-project-325743) has also been updated for Unity 6.1.

## For Unity for Industry

In 2025, we began expanding our technical content to support Unity for Industry, with a focus on our powerful templates that provide a rapid start for common use cases.

### Build web-based product configurators fast

If you’re looking for tips on how to create polished and engaging web-based product configurators, start with our [Unity Product Configurator tutorial video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TF10tyOMQ30) and its companion article [Build a simple product configurator in Unity in one hour or less](https://unity.com/resources/build-product-configurator-unity-industry).

And, speaking of XR, our [Introduction to XR for Industry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jUft9GZ-EI4&t=2s) video will jumpstart your XR production using a selection of our best starter assets.

## For live ops and monetizing your game

### The Unity Gaming Services 10-part tutorial series

Each tutorial in this 10-part mega series consists of [a video on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLX2vGYjWbI0TNcNNSaUJwC0onmoQrT-Li) and [an article on Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unity-gaming-services-tutorial-series-part-1-introduction/1685294). You’ll learn how to build a base project that handles [authentication](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unity-gaming-services-tutorial-series-part-2-player-login/1689527), inventories, and monetization. Additionally, you’ll get line-by-line instructions for how to integrate services like Cloud Code, Cloud Save, rewarded ads, in-app purchases and many more.

Unity Gaming Services tutorial series

*]:text-body [&>*]:mb-4 [&>h3]:font-semibold">

You can also see these services in action in a new sample project on GitHub, [Gem Hunter Match – Cloud Edition (GHM – CE)](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.services.samples.use-cases/tree/main/GemHunterMatch3). The demo project is aimed at advanced Unity developers who have solid experience with core Unity development, but have yet to use UGS features in a functional game and are looking for tips on how to do so.

Gem Hunter Match sample screens in the Unity Gaming Services tutorial series
